CRGD welcomes contributions from researchers, practitioners, students and citizens who have something useful to say about public policy. We are particularly keen to hear from people with direct experience of the issues they write about.

## What we accept

| Series | Who it is for | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Expert View](/publications/expert-view) | Practitioners and scholars with long experience in a field | 1,200 to 2,000 words |
| [Opinion](/publications/opinion) | Anyone with a well argued view on a policy question | 800 to 1,200 words |
| [Commentary](/publications/commentary) | Timely responses to policy developments | 600 to 1,000 words |
| [Reviews](/publications/reviews) | Reviews of books, reports and policy documents | 800 to 1,500 words |
| [Young Thoughts](/publications/young-thoughts) | Students and people under 25, including campus club members | 600 to 1,200 words |
| [Blogs](/publications/blogs) | Field notes, reflections and short analysis | 500 to 1,000 words |

We do not normally publish unsolicited full length research reports, but we are happy to discuss collaboration on research ideas.

## How to submit

1. **Send a pitch first.** Email a short pitch of no more than 200 words to the submissions address below. Tell us the argument, why it matters now, and why you are the right person to write it.
2. **We reply within ten working days.** If we are interested, an editor will agree length, deadline and focus with you.
3. **Write and submit the draft** as an editable document, with sources linked or listed at the end.
4. **Editing.** An editor will work with you on structure, clarity and accuracy. You will approve the final version before publication.

Send pitches to [{{submissionsEmail}}](mailto:{{submissionsEmail}}) with "Pitch:" and your proposed title in the subject line.

## Our expectations

- The piece must be your original work and not published elsewhere, unless agreed in advance.
- Factual claims must be supported by sources we can check.
- Disclose any interest that a reader would consider relevant, such as employment, funding or political affiliation.
- Write in plain language. Avoid jargon and acronyms where you can.
- Respect people you disagree with. We do not publish personal attacks.

## Young Thoughts

The Young Thoughts series is mentored. Student writers are paired with a CRGD researcher who helps them develop their argument and their evidence. It is a good place for a first publication. Campus club members can also submit through their club coordinator.
