Every CRGD project follows the same broad path. It is not a bureaucratic checklist, but a way of making sure that what we publish is worth reading and worth acting on.

## 1. Choose questions that matter

We start with questions that people who make or live with policy are actually asking. Ideas come from our researchers, from officials and legislators, from civil society organisations, from our campus networks and from the public. Each research vertical keeps a short list of priority questions, published on its page.

## 2. Look at what is already known

Before collecting new data, we review existing research, official statistics, audit reports, court judgments and experience from other states and countries. Often, the most useful contribution is to bring scattered evidence together.

## 3. Use the right methods

We choose methods to fit the question: statistical analysis, surveys, interviews, field observation, legal analysis, budget analysis, comparative case studies or modelling. We say clearly what our methods can and cannot show.

## 4. Listen to the people affected

For any study that leads to recommendations, we consult people who would be affected by them, including those who are usually unheard. We report what we heard, including views we do not share.

## 5. Check before we publish

Every substantial publication is reviewed internally, and major reports are reviewed by at least one expert outside CRGD. Our [research standards](/research-standards) set out the process in detail.

## 6. Write for the reader

We write in plain language. We put conclusions first, explain technical terms and show our evidence. Long reports come with short briefs; data comes with explanations.

## 7. Follow through

After publication, we brief decision makers, talk to the media, and track what happens. We record outcomes, including failures, on our [Impact](/impact) pages.

## Our values in practice

- **Independence:** funders have no say in our conclusions. See our [independence and funding policy](/independence-and-funding).
- **Non partisanship:** we work with and criticise governments of every political colour.
- **Openness:** we publish methods and, where we can, data.
- **Respect:** we protect the privacy and dignity of the people who take part in our research.
- **Correction:** when we get something wrong, we say so publicly. See our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy).
