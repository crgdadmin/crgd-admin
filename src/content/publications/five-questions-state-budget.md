Every year the budget speech dominates the news for a day. New schemes are announced, allocations are praised or criticised, and then attention moves on. The documents that accompany the speech, often running to thousands of pages, receive much less attention. They contain the answers to five questions that matter more than any announcement.

## 1. How much of the revenue is already spoken for?

Look at **committed expenditure**: salaries, pensions and interest payments. When these absorb most of the state's revenue receipts, there is little room for new priorities, whatever the speech says. Track this share over several years.

## 2. Is the state borrowing to invest or to pay bills?

Compare the **revenue deficit** with the **fiscal deficit**. Borrowing to build assets that will serve future generations is defensible. Borrowing to meet day to day expenses passes the bill to the future without leaving anything behind.

## 3. What happened to last year's promises?

Compare last year's **budget estimates** with the **revised estimates** and, when they are available, the **actuals**. Large gaps, especially in capital spending, show where announcements were not followed by action.

## 4. Where is the money going, not just how much?

Look beyond totals to the **composition of spending** within departments. A larger health budget may be mostly salaries, or mostly new buildings, or mostly medicines. Each tells a different story.

## 5. What is outside the budget?

Find the statements on **guarantees** and on borrowing by **state owned bodies**. These obligations may not appear in the headline deficit but can fall on the budget in future.

## Using these questions

None of these questions requires specialist training. They require time and patience with the budget documents, which are published online. CRGD publishes a plain language reading of the state budget every year that works through these questions.
