One of the hardest decisions a research institution makes is what not to study. Every week we hear of problems that deserve attention. Our capacity is limited, and a half finished study helps no one. So we use a few simple tests.

**Does someone need the answer?** We prefer questions that a decision maker, an institution or a community is actually facing, where better evidence could change what they do.

**Can we add something?** If good research already exists, our job may be to bring it to the right people rather than to repeat it. We look for gaps.

**Can we do it well?** We ask whether we have, or can bring in, the skills and access the question requires.

**Is the timing right?** Some questions are urgent because a decision is about to be made. Others are slow and structural. We try to keep a balance between the two.

**Will it be heard?** A study that no one will read is a poor use of time. We think about who the audience is from the start.

Questions that pass these tests go to the relevant research vertical, which keeps a short list of priorities published on its page.

We welcome suggestions. Some of our best projects began with a message from a reader, an official or a student. If you have a question you think we should be asking, [tell us](/contact).
