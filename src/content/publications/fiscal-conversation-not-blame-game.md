Every discussion of Kerala's public finances seems to follow the same script. The government blames the Union for reduced transfers and borrowing limits. The opposition blames the government for overspending and debt. Each side is partly right. Neither conversation helps a citizen understand the choices the state actually faces.

Those choices are real and difficult. The state has commitments to its employees and pensioners that it must honour. It has public services that citizens value and expect to improve. It has infrastructure needs that will not wait. It has a population that is ageing, which will raise health and pension costs further. And it has a revenue base that, for many reasons, has not grown as fast as those commitments.

A grown up conversation would start from shared facts: how much the state raises, how much it spends and on what, how much it owes and when that debt falls due. It would then ask what the options are, and who would bear the cost of each. Raise more revenue, and from whom? Spend less, and on what? Borrow more, and for what purpose? Ask more of the Union, and on what grounds?

There are no painless answers. But a public that understands the choices is more likely to accept the decisions that follow, and more likely to hold its leaders to account for them. Blame is easy. Choices are harder, and they are what governing is about.
