India's Constitution divides power between the Union and the states, and since 1992 has given a constitutional place to panchayats and municipalities. How that division works in practice is one of the most consequential and least understood questions in Indian public life.

## Why it matters

Kerala raises a large share of its own revenue but depends on transfers for much of the rest. The rules for those transfers, the design of the Goods and Services Tax, conditions attached to central schemes and limits on state borrowing all shape what the state government can do. Kerala is also known for decentralising real power and money to local governments, an experiment that offers lessons and unfinished business.
