Much of Kerala is neither clearly urban nor clearly rural. Census figures over recent decades have shown a large rise in places with urban characteristics that are still governed as panchayats. This continuum creates particular challenges for planning and services.

## Why it matters

Waste management failures, water shortages, flooding in towns and rising housing costs are urban problems that affect most of the state's population. Municipal governments have limited revenue and capacity. Planning laws were largely designed for a sharper distinction between town and country.

## How we work

- **Settlement studies.** We map how places are changing and how they are governed.
- **Service delivery.** We study water, sanitation and waste systems in specific towns and panchayats.
- **Municipal finance.** We analyse property tax, user charges and grants.
- **Housing.** We study housing demand, rental markets and public housing programs.

The vertical works closely with Mobility, Environment and Federalism, and with elected representatives in urban local bodies.
