Every policy promise eventually becomes a line in a budget. This vertical follows the money: where it comes from, where it goes, and whether anyone can tell what it achieved.

## Why it matters

Kerala built strong public services on the back of steady public spending, but a large share of today's revenue is committed before the year begins, to salaries, pensions and interest. Choices about capital investment, welfare and debt now carry consequences for decades. Citizens and legislators deserve clear, independent analysis of those choices, not slogans from either side.

Accountability is the other half of the story. Audit reports, legislative committees, the right to information and social audits all exist on paper. The question this vertical asks is how well they work in practice, and what would make them work better.

## How we work

- **Budget analysis.** Each year we publish a plain language reading of the Kerala budget and of relevant Union budget decisions within weeks of their presentation.
- **Follow the audit.** We track selected audit findings over time to see which are acted upon.
- **Local finance.** With partner panchayats and municipalities, we study how decentralised planning funds are planned, spent and reported.
- **Administrative reform.** We document reforms that worked elsewhere in India and abroad, and test whether they would travel.

The vertical works closely with the Federalism, Economy and Institutions and Social Justice verticals.
